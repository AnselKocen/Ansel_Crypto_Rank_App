# Currency Evolution Timeline

## 1. First Metal Money (1000 B.C.)

### Origin
- **Location**: China (end of Stone Age)
- **Forms**: Bronze and Copper cowrie imitations; Metal tool money (knife and spade monies)

### Evolution
- Developed into primitive round coins
- Made of base metals
- Often contained holes to be strung like a chain

### Significance
- First standardized metal currency
- Transition from barter to uniform monetary units

---

## 2. Modern Coinage (500 B.C.)

### Origin
- **Location**: Lydia (present-day Turkey)
- **Development**: Independent from Chinese monetary evolution

### Material Characteristics
- Started as lumps of silver
- Evolved into round coins made of precious metals (silver, bronze, gold)
- Possessed inherent value based on metal content

### Features
- Stamped with gods and emperors to mark authenticity
- Standardized weights and measurements

### Geographic Spread
- Techniques copied by Greek, Persian, Macedonian, and Roman empires
- Foundation for Western monetary systems

---

## 3. Leather Money (118 B.C.)

### Form
- **Description**: One-foot-square pieces of white deerskin with colorful borders
- **Origin**: China

### Significance
- Considered the first documented type of banknote
- Early representative currency (not commodity-based)
- Precursor to paper money

---

## 4. Paper Currency (806 A.D.)

### Origin
- **Location**: China
- **First appearance**: 9th century

### Duration and Usage
- Over 500 years of continuous use (9th-15th century)
- First widespread paper banknote system

### Economic Impact
- **Production growth**: Increased printing capacity
- **Value depreciation**: Oversupply led to reduced value
- **Inflation**: Prices soared as currency devalued

### Disappearance and Reappearance
- **Ended**: 1455 (abandoned for several hundred years)
- **European emergence**: Reappeared later in Europe
- **Global adoption**: Eventually became worldwide standard

---

## 5. The Gold Standard (1816)

### Establishment
- **Year**: 1816
- **Location**: England
- **Action**: Gold officially made standard of value

### Mechanism
- Guidelines for non-inflationary production of banknotes
- Each note represented specific gold amounts
- Convertibility guaranteed by central authority

### US Adoption
- **Legislation**: Gold Standard Act enacted in 1900
- **Impact**: Aided central bank establishment
- **Result**: Stabilized international trade

---

## 6. End of Gold Standard (1930)

### Cause
- Massive Depression of the 1930s
- Economic crisis required monetary flexibility

### Process
- **US action**: Revised standard and devalued gold price
- **First step**: Beginning of ending direct gold relationship
- **International follow-up**: British and other nations abandoned standard

### Result
- End of direct gold worth relationship
- Rise of complex international monetary regulation
- Transition to fiat currency systems

---

## 7. Electronic Money (Future)

### Mechanism
- Economic transactions via bits and bytes
- No physical currency exchange required
- Digital representation of value

### Characteristics
- Instantaneous transfer capability
- Global accessibility
- Reduced transaction costs

### Projection
- Expected to be the dominant currency of the future
- Continues evolution toward fully digital monetary systems

---

## Regulatory Milestones

- **Gold Standard Act (1900)**: US formal adoption of gold backing
- **End of Gold Standard (1930)**: Transition to fiat currency systems

## Domain Connections

- **Numismatics**: Study of physical currency forms and evolution
- **Banking**: Development of institutions to manage currency systems
- **Monetary Policy**: Government regulation of money supply and value
- **Fintech**: Modern digital currency and payment technologies
