# Cryptocurrency Fundamentals and Classification

## When to Use
- Evaluating an asset's suitability or effectiveness as a form of money
- Categorizing a cryptocurrency asset
- Analyzing an Initial Coin Offering (ICO)
- Understanding the difference between coins and tokens

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of basic economic concepts of value and exchange
- Knowledge of Blockchain technology
- Understanding of Open Source Code

## Evaluation of Money Types (5 Characteristics)

Compare the asset against the following 5 characteristics:

### 1. Scarcity (Supply limitations)
| Asset | Scarcity | Explanation |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| Fiat Money | NO | Governments can print more daily; value falls as supply increases |
| Gold | YES | Value comes from scarcity; mining is becoming difficult and supply is running out |
| Bitcoin | YES | Hard-capped at 21 million coins; no more can be created |

### 2. Divisible (Ability to split into smaller amounts)
| Asset | Divisible | Explanation |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| Fiat Money | YES | Easily divisible |
| Gold | NO | Difficult to divide for small transactions (e.g., paying for a cup of tea) |
| Bitcoin | YES | Highly divisible; one 'Satoshi' equals one hundred millionth of a Bitcoin |

### 3. Transportable (Ease of transfer)
| Asset | Transportable | Explanation |
|-------|--------------|-------------|
| Fiat Money | YES (with caveats) | YES via electronic systems, BUT it is expensive and slow |
| Gold | NO | Difficult to send overseas in bulk |
| Bitcoin | YES | Can be sent like a digital file to another wallet within minutes for a fraction of the price |

### 4. Durable (Ability to withstand wear/destruction)
| Asset | Durable | Explanation |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| Fiat Money | SOME | Coins are durable, but paper notes are not; new notes must be printed regularly |
| Gold | YES | It is extremely durable |
| Bitcoin | YES | A Bitcoin cannot be destroyed |

### 5. Recognisable (Acceptance and verification)
| Asset | Recognisable | Explanation |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Fiat Money | Mixed | YES within the country of issue. NO internationally (requires exchange). Prone to counterfeiting |
| Gold | Mixed | YES - some countries prefer its safety. Verification challenge: difficult to confirm if received gold is real |
| Bitcoin | Mixed | YES - if you have a wallet, you can hold it. NO (currently) - majority of shops do not accept it yet. Cannot be copied or counterfeited |

## Classification: Coin vs Token

### Decision Tree

1. **Analyze the asset's origin and codebase**:
   - Is it a variation of Bitcoin's open source code?
   - Does it operate on its own native blockchain (or a fork of one)?

2. **IF YES**: Classify as a **Coin (or Altcoin)**
   - **Utility**: Acts as a simple store of value or digital money
   - **Functionality**: Limited to one primary utility (value transfer/storage). You cannot build complex applications on it
   - **Examples**: Litecoin, Dogecoin, Bitcoin Cash, Bitcoin Gold
   - **Market Terminology**: The launch event is called an "ICO"

3. **IF NO (Secondary Asset)**: Classify as a **Token**
   - **Ecosystem**: Usually exists within the Ethereum blockchain system (or similar programmable blockchains)
   - **Purpose**: Secondary asset used to run a specific Decentralised Application (DApp)
   - **Generation**: Created by a Smart Contract System (SCS)
   - **Utility**: Highly programmable with multi-functionality. Can store complex, multi-faceted levels of value
   - **Examples**: GNT (Golem), GNO (Gnosis)
   - **Market Terminology**: The launch event is called a "Token Launch"

### Summary of Difference
- **Coins** = Simple money/value store (Bitcoin produces coins)
- **Tokens** = Programmable assets for complex applications (Ethereum generates tokens)

## Constraints
- Does not apply to assets not intended to be used as a medium of exchange or store of value
- Distinction between Coin and Token is based on technical architecture and utility, not market cap or price
