# Early Money Systems - Historical Examples

## 1. Barter (The Beginning)

### Definition
Exchange of resources or services for mutual advantage between parties.

### Historical Context
- **Timeline**: Dates back tens of thousands of years, potentially to the dawn of modern humans
- **Scope**: Not purely human; observed in plants and animals through symbiotic relationships
- **Precedence**: Pre-dates all formal money systems
- **Current Status**: Still used today by individuals, organizations, and governments

### Key Characteristics
- Direct exchange without intermediary medium
- Requires double coincidence of wants
- Based on mutual perceived advantage

---

## 2. Cattle (9000 - 6000 B.C.)

### Status
First and oldest form of money in human history.

### Composition
- Cows
- Sheep
- Camels
- Other livestock globally

### Historical Context
- **Period**: 9000 - 6000 B.C.
- **Agricultural Link**: Advent of agriculture introduced grain and vegetable/plant products as standard barter items
- **Geographic Spread**: Used across multiple agricultural societies

### Significance
- Represents the transition from hunter-gatherer to agricultural economies
- Livestock served as store of value, medium of exchange, and unit of account

---

## 3. Cowrie Shells (1200 B.C.)

### Origin
- Shells of a mollusc from Pacific and Indian Oceans
- First used in China

### Historical Context
- **Timeline**: 1200 B.C. onwards
- **Geographic Spread**: Used by many societies across Asia, Africa, and Pacific islands
- **Duration**: Used in parts of Africa until the mid-20th century

### Significance
- **Most widely and longest used currency in history**
- Durable, portable, and difficult to counterfeit
- Standardized size and shape made them ideal as money

---

## 4. Potlach (1500)

### Origin
Chinook Indian custom in North American Indian cultures.

### Nature
Ceremony involving:
- Gift exchange
- Dances
- Feasts
- Public rituals

### Social Function
- Form of initiation into secret tribal societies
- Crucial for establishing a leader's social rank
- Reinforced social hierarchy and status

### Dynamics
- Often spiralled out of control as gifts and feasts became more lavish
- Competitive displays of generosity to out-do others
- Could lead to resource depletion in competitive scenarios

### Historical Context
- **Timeline**: Documented from 1500, likely existed earlier
- **Cultural Context**: Integral to Pacific Northwest tribal societies

---

## 5. Wampum (1535)

### Composition
Strings of beads made from clam shells.

### Etymology
Indian word "wampum" means white (the color of the beads).

### Historical Context
- **Earliest Known Use**: 1535 by North American Indians
- **Actual Origin**: Likely existed earlier than documented
- **Geographic Region**: Northeastern North America

### Characteristics
- Crafted from quahog clam shells
- White and purple beads had different values
- Used for trade, tribute, and record-keeping
- Served as both currency and cultural artifact
