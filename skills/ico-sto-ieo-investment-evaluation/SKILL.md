# ICO/STO/IEO Investment Evaluation

## When to Use
- A blockchain startup seeks funding
- An investor seeks to participate in a crypto crowdsale
- Evaluating whether to invest in a crypto startup

## Prerequisites
- Possession of liquid cryptocurrencies (e.g., Bitcoin, Ether)
- Ability to read and evaluate technical white papers
- Risk tolerance for high-risk, unregulated investments

## Risk Assessment
- High risk of total capital loss
- Lack of regulation for ICOs and IEOs makes fund recovery difficult
- Projects are in very early stages
- Never invest funds that cannot be completely lost

## Fundraising Model Identification

| Model | Regulation | Description |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| **ICO** (Initial Coin Offering) | Unregulated | Crowdsale trading future crypto coins for immediate liquid cryptocurrencies (BTC, ETH). Occurs before blockchain launch. |
| **STO** (Security Token Offering) | Regulated | Closest to an IPO. Security tokens represent ownership of the company (e.g., 1% tokens = 1% ownership). Faster than IPO but requires similar regulation. |
| **IEO** (Initial Exchange Offering) | Unregulated | Exchange (e.g., Binance) launches the coin through its platform. Investors rely on exchange's due diligence, but personal research remains critical. |

## Asset Classification

### Coins
- Native cryptocurrencies used for transferring monetary value (e.g., Bitcoin)
- Simple store of value or digital money
- Limited to one primary utility (value transfer/storage)

### Tokens
- Assets that store complex and multifaceted data streams
- Usually exist within Ethereum blockchain system
- Created by Smart Contract System
- Highly programmable with multi-functionality
- Evaluate based on business model and potential solution

## White Paper Evaluation
Verify the project has a white paper detailing:
- How the technology is intended to work
- Token design, acquisition methods, and usage
- The specific problem being solved and the solution mechanism
- How the tokens are utilized to solve the problem (crucial for investor value)

## Comprehensive Due Diligence Framework

### 1. Team Evaluation (Experience & Reputation)
- Read white paper and analyze founding members and team profiles on LinkedIn
- Verify history in the space and specific crypto experience (previous projects/ICOs and impact)
- Visit BitcoinTalk.org to gauge sentiment from experienced investors; look for keywords like 'scam'
- Check if developers avoid answering questions or lack collaboration
- Send a personal message to test responsiveness
- **Decision Point**: If the team cannot deliver based on experience, do not continue

### 2. Idea Validation (Problem & Market)
- Determine if the idea solves a real problem and has a potential hungry market
- Assess if the project brings unique value to the world
- Check if it is a 'me-too' product without significant improvement over existing solutions
- **Decision Point**: Do not invest unless the company can do it much better than competitors

### 3. Token Utility Analysis
- Identify the specific purpose of the new dedicated token
- **Critical Question**: Why isn't Bitcoin or Ethereum sufficient for this project?

### 4. Financial Structure & Cap
- **Cap Analysis**: Be wary of unlimited/open caps (unlimited funding leads to less unique tokens and less demand)
- **Fund Allocation**: Verify transparency on where money goes (Development budget %, Marketing budget %, other allocations)

### 5. Token Value & Distribution
- **Value Assessment**: Evaluate if current value is worth it and if growth potential exists
- **Saturation Check**: Determine if there are too many tokens on offer which will saturate the market
- **Incentives**: Verify if there are real incentives to use the token
- **Distribution Schedule**: Check team allocation (Greed indicator: >50% to team is suspicious)
- **Roadmap Linkage**: Ensure distribution is linked to project phases/milestones (e.g., beta version release)

## Operational Execution Steps
1. Startup advertises the sale of the initial coin supply
2. Investors read the white paper and exchange Bitcoin or Ether for the new coins
3. Startup converts the received Bitcoin or Ether into fiat currency to fund technology development and costs
4. If the project is successful (launches and gains adoption), the value of the new currency rises, allowing investors to make a profit
